Detailed synopsis of The Richest Man in Babylon

7 CURES FOR A LEAN PURSE

The chapter begins with the king of Babylon, Sargon, inquiring with his royal chancellor.
He is confused and concerned about the lack of wealth among the common people despite the many years of prosperity created by the irrigation systems,
temples, and other endeavors. Still, many subjects were unable to have enough to survive outside of a few rich men. After the chancellor explains that
the people simply do not know how the king sends for the richest man in Babylon, Akrad.The King asks Akrad to teach some individuals how to attain wealth
so that they may teach others, and he accepts. In the first class, Akrad uses the time to get acquainted with his students and their jobs and begins to teach
what he calls the 7 cures for a lean purse.The first cure is “For every ten coins thou placest within thy purse take out far use but nine”. “Thy purse will start
to fatten at once, and its increasing weight will feel good in thy hand and bring satisfaction to thy soul.” This means that instead of using all of your money. Start
leaving some in, no matter the amount, and slowly but surely you will start to build wealth.

The second class, Akrad, talks about how some of the members have asked how they can keep one-tenth if they don't earn enough for their necessary expenses.
He explains that many tend to confuse necessary expenses with desired wants and that it is important to limit yourself as well as to be able to tell what is
a need and what is a want. As he says, “As weeds grow in a field wherever the farmer leaves space for their roots, even so freely desires grow in men whenever
there is a possibility of their being gratified.” He explains the importance of budgeting, as well as not touching the one-tenth you saved during the first cure.
The second cure is “Budget thy expenses that thou mayest have coins to pay for thy necessities, to pay for thy enjoyments and to gratify thy worthwhile desires without
spending more than nine-tenths of thy earnings.

The Third class is about using your new growing wealth and investing it properly. Akard speaks about his first profitable investment being giving loans to a shield maker called Aggar,
and over time, each time Aggar repaid the loan, Akard gained more wealth. And after he gained more continued to loan others creating multiple sources of income. He also spoke about
giving money to moneylenders and talked about a farmer who gave 2 pieces of silver to a lender for his firstborn son, and in the end the son gained a large sum of money from this investment
when he became older. Supporting the 3rd cure. “To put each coin into labouring that it may reproduce its kind even as the flacks of the field and help bring to thee income a stream of wealth
that shall flow constantly into thy purse”.

In the fourth class, He explains the importance of protecting one's wealth. And to be wary of investing all your money into opportunities that seem too good to be true.
And to be wary of who you loan money to invest in. He speaks of his first ever investment, which went wrong. In which he gave money to a brickmaker named Azmur, whom he
sent to bring back rare jewels from the Phoenicians so they could sell them and divide the profits. Azmur was swindled by the Phoenicians, who sold him bits of glass instead.
He instructed them to seek wisdom and advice from others who are more experienced instead of purely relying on their own wisdom. Which then he tells them the 4th cure.
“Guard thy treasure from loss by investing only where thy principal is safe, where it may be reclaimed if desirable, and where thou will not fail to collect a fair rental.
Consult with wisemen. Secure the advice of those experienced in the profitable handling of gold. Let their wisdom protect thy treasure from unsafe investment”.

In the fifth class, he speaks about the living conditions many in Babylon live in and talks about investing in buying land and houses. So that their children may play safely
and their wives may farm and plant flowers.And the satisfaction that comes with owning your own land and growing your own food.With this cure being the shortest: “Own thy Own Home”.

In the sixth class, he informs them of the importance of setting up safeguards in case they may die early. To ensure that their families are left supported in their absence.
Instead of hiding money inside the house, they should invest in assets that can either generate funds or can be sold in the future, for example, land, businesses, and homes.
Or give money to a moneylender to increase. He also spoke about his wish for a system that allows for money to be given to the family in cases of death. He, of course, is talking
about the modern day insurance system, which did not exist at the time. The sixth cure is “Provide in advance for the needs of thy growing age and the protection of thy family.

The seventh class he spoke about a young man who came up to him to borrow money but did not have a sufficient income to be able to pay him back. After talking with the man,
he learned that the man had tried to ask for a pay increase numerous times but kept getting rejected.He spoke about how it is important to have tangible desires that are simple
and definite that you can focus on. And that the more you work, the more you know, and the more skilled you are, the more you will be paid. And it is important to seek guidance
and wisdom to improve yourself in your job or craft.He says, “Many things come to make a man's life rich with gainful experiences, such things as the following a man must do if he
respects himself: He must pay his debts with all the promptness within his power, not purchasing that for which he is unable to pay.He must make a will of record that, in case the
gods called him, proper and honourable division of his property be accomplished; he must have compassion upon those who are injured and smitten by misfortune and aid them within reasonable limits.
One must do deeds of thoughtfulness to those dear to him” .The final cure is “to cultivate thy own powers to study and become wiser. To become more skillful, to so act to respect thyself.”
He then sends them on their way to spread the teachings and educate the masses.

Detailed synopsis of The Richest Man in Babylon

7 LAWS OF GOLD

This chapter is about an old merchant named Kalabad and the twenty seven workers he employed to secure and protect his cargo as they journey across the desert to Babylon.
Around a campfire, he asks them to choose between a bag of gold and a clay tablet with words of wisdom. After hearing their answer, he tells them the tale of the 5 Laws of Gold
that were told to him by the son of Akrad, the richest man in Babylon, Nomasir. The story goes that when Nomasir was approaching adulthood. Akrad wanted to make sure that his son
was capable and worthy to take over his estate. He gave his son a bag of gold and a clay tablet with words of wisdom on it and tasked him to make something of himself within ten years.

Nomasir sets out to the developing city of Nineveh and gets scammed and swindled twice. The first time by two men who owned white horses. They spoke of a wealthy man who claimed to have
the fastest horse in all of Babylon and had put up a prize for whoever could find a horse faster than his. He, of course, failed and then found out the men were working with the wealthy man,
who tricked individuals and split the profits. The second swindle came from a fellow like himself, who was a son of a wealthy man. He spoke of a merchant who had died and left behind his shop
filled with merchandise. He claimed they would be equal partners, but that he had forgotten his gold in Babylon and so asked Namour to purchase the building, and he would repay him. After getting
tired of his antics, Namour kicked him out of the partnership, but by the time he gained the merchandise, the quality had diminished, and it had to be sold for a lower price than it was originally
sold for. Because of these failures, he ended up losing most of his gold.

No matter where he went, he could not keep a job because he lacked the skill and experience. Then he remembers the clay tablet given to him by his father . Where he reads the 5 laws of gold:
1. Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that or his family.
2. Gold laboureth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.
3. Gold clingeth to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.
4. Gold slippeth away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.
5. Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusts it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.

With these laws, he was able to work, managing slaves and building the outwall under a wise man. The man seeing how dedicated to his work started to befriend and give him wisdom including putting him in on great investment plans.
Soon, Nomair, with aid from his wise friends and masters, was able to steadily grow wealth. Enough so that he came back to his family. They greeted him with a large feast and celebration, and he told them about his journey and thanked
his father for his words of wisdom and for the experience he gained. In the end, his father was proud of his achievements and welcomed him as the heir to his fortune.

This story talks about wisdom. And how money without wisdom is worthless, and only in educating oneself using past experiences can one truly learn and grow.

As someone who struggles with saving money, I believe that these chapter provides timeless and helpful information to help individuals even in the modern world.
